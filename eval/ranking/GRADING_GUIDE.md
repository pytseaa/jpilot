# Grading guide (DRAFT, for the team to edit before any rating starts)

You see one CV and a list of jobs. You do not know which system picked a job. Grade each job on **how well the CV fits the job ad**, nothing else.

Ignore: how senior the person wants to be, which industry they like, whether the ad looks good or the company is famous, and how the job was found.

| Grade | Meaning | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Not a fit | The CV does not show what the ad mainly asks for. A different field or level, or a must-have skill is missing |
| **1** | Plausible fit | The CV matches some of what the ad asks for, but a main requirement is missing, weak or unclear. You could imagine applying, with gaps |
| **2** | Strong fit | The CV clearly shows most of what the ad asks for, including the main requirements. You would expect an interview |

## How to decide

1. Read the ad's **main** requirements (job type, years or level, key skills, language).
2. Look for each one in the CV. Count only what the CV actually says.
3. Pick the grade from the table. If you are torn between two, take the **lower** one.

## Examples (the team must replace these with 2 real ones per grade from the snapshot, written before any system runs)

These made-up examples show the idea only.

- **0:** CV of a pastry chef, ad for a software developer. Nothing matches.
- **1:** CV with 3 years of customer service, ad for a customer service officer that asks for 5 years and fluent Mandarin. The CV shows the work but not the years, and Mandarin is not mentioned.
- **2:** CV of a logistics officer with 9 years in freight forwarding, ad for a logistics officer that asks for 5 years of freight and customs documents. Both are on the CV.

## Rules for the two raters

- Rate on your own. Do not talk until both files are done.
- Do not look up which system produced a job. The files do not say.
- Nobody rates a CV they wrote. (The CVs in `eval/cvs/` have no named author yet. Fill in `author` in `settings.json` first.)
- After both files are done, the team records the agreement (weighted kappa), then you talk **only** about jobs you disagreed on. If you still disagree after talking, the job gets the **lower** grade, so a 2 means both of you said 2.
